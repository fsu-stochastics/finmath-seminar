Sep 10, 2026 Lingjiong Zhu
Florida State University		 Optimal Portfolio Choice with Diffusion-Based Data Augmentation
Portfolio selection rules rely on estimated parameters, whose errors, driven by limited sample sizes, often erode realized performance. We propose augmenting observed data with synthetic samples generated by diffusion models. We show theoretically that the data-augmented rule dominates the standard plug-in rule in expected utility, and empirically, using simulated and real market data, find that augmentation substantially improves out-of-sample performance across every rule examined. This offers a practical tool for mitigating estimation risk, pointing to a promising role for generative AI in decision-making under uncertainty, including in predictions and sentiment measures.

Sep 17, 2026 Qijin Shi
UCSB		 No-arbitrage with Rough Paths: A Collapse Toward the Classical Setting
Rough path theory extends stochastic calculus to irregular processes beyond the classical semimartingale framework. We investigate whether this broader calculus also permits a broader theory of frictionless financial markets without arbitrage. For markets with gains specified by rough integration, we formulate a No Controlled Free Lunch condition and use a Kreps–Yan-type theorem to characterize it through zero expected gains under an equivalent measure. This leads to the study of unbiased rough integrators. We establish classification results for one-dimensional rough paths under suitable regularity and integrability assumptions. As the permitted strategies expand from state-dependent rules to signature-based portfolios incorporating past information, the classification becomes increasingly restrictive: Gaussian marginals and a Hermite structure give way to a Brownian Itô lift with a deterministic change of time. Thus, within this framework, sufficiently rich continuous trading brings the model back to the classical semimartingale setting.

Sep 24, 2026 Qi Feng
Florida State University		 Title: Continuous Policy and Value Iteration for Relaxed Stochastic Control, and beyond.
Abstract

We introduce a continuous policy-value iteration algorithm where the approximations of the value function of a stochastic control problem and the optimal control are simultaneously updated through Langevin-type dynamics. This paper focuses on the entropy-regularized relaxed control problem with infinite horizon. We establish policy improvement and demonstrate convergence to the optimal control under the monotonicity condition of the Hamiltonian. By utilizing Langevin-type stochastic differential equations for continuous updates along the policy iteration direction, our approach enables the use of distribution sampling techniques in machine learning to optimize the value function and identify the optimal control simultaneously. Numerical experiments are presented for LQ model with a non-concave reward, and a non-LQ example with a skewed-Laplace optimal control. Towards the end, I will present our new results on score-Hamiltonian policy iteration algorithms.
Oct 1, 2026 Brayton Link
Florida State University		 Signature Kernel Learning for PDEs
Abstract

Partial differential equations (PDEs) provide a fundamental framework for modeling systems that evolve in both space and time, but finding their solution can become challenging when the underlying forcing or coefficient functions are known only through discrete or sequential observations. This work investigates the use of signature kernels as a machine learning framework for approximating solutions to such PDEs. Signature kernels arise from the path signature, which represents sequential data through a hierarchy of iterated integrals that capture increasingly complex interactions along a path. We focus on inhomogeneous PDEs in which the forcing function is observed along spatial and temporal trajectories. These trajectories are embedded into reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces through the signature kernel, allowing the unknown solution to be approximated using finite kernel expansions. In particular, we study the inhomogeneous heat and wave equations as a model problem and construct approximations of its solution using signature-kernel Gram matrices generated from sampled forcing data. The resulting framework combines the structure of the governing differential equation with data-driven representations of sequential observations, providing a connection between signature methods, kernel-based machine learning, and numerical PDE approximation.
Oct 8, 2026 David Herzog
Iowa State University		 Title: Quantitative positivity of transition densities for random perturbations of Hamiltonian systems
Abstract: I will present recent results on a class of diffusion processes arising from random perturbations of conservative Hamiltonian systems. Under a set of abstract hypotheses---including basic structural assumptions on the Hamiltonian, a weak Lyapunov structure, and a quantitative notion of smoothing---we prove that transition densities satisfy a sharp, uniform pointwise lower bound over Hamiltonian sublevel sets in the small noise limit. By applying our general theorem, we obtain quantitative minorization estimates for a variety of models including Langevin dynamics, chains of oscillators coupled to heat bathes at different temperatures, and finite-dimensional fluid models such as stochastically forced Galerkin truncations of the Navier-Stokes equations and the Lorenz '96 system. As a corollary, assuming a stronger Lyapunov structure, our main result yields a sharp exponential rate of convergence to equilibrium in a weighted total variation norm. A central feature of our approach is that it does not require knowledge of the explicit form of the invariant measure, nor even its existence, and hence is broadly applicable to deduce minorization for physically relevant systems where invariant measures are inaccessible.

Oct 22, 2026 Haoyang Cao
Johns Hopkins University		 Title: Randomized Impulse Control Problem and Reinforcement Learning
Abstract

In this work, we study a randomized optimal impulse control problem. This framework is particularly suitable for devising reinforcement learning algorithms with exploration to solve the otherwise hard-to-explicitly-derive optimal impulse control policies. We provide a two-fold randomized impulse control framework and then analyze the associated HJB equation. We discuss its relations with (randomized) optimal stopping as well as the classical impulse control problem. We also present an exploratory reinforcement learning algorithm to learn the randomized impulse policy. This is a joint work with Prof. Yuchao Dong (Tongji University) and Zhouhao Yang (Johns Hopkins University).
